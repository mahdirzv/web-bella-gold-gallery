# Jewellery Website Research and Recommended Information Architecture

Research date: 31 August 2026

## Executive recommendation

The strongest jewellery sites combine two jobs:

1. **Create desire** with a restrained campaign, excellent photography and a recognisable point of view.
2. **Remove buying uncertainty** with clear category routes, complete product information, sizing, materials, delivery, returns, care and human service.

The recommended direction is therefore **editorial luxury with disciplined commerce**. Keep the Warm Editorial visual character, but organise the real website around conventional, predictable shopping behaviour. The homepage should not be a long art piece or a product grid from the first pixel; it should lead from campaign to discovery to proof.

## What successful jewellery websites have in common

- A short announcement bar with one current message.
- A strong global header with category navigation, search, account, wishlist and bag.
- One campaign-led hero with a single primary action.
- Immediate routes by product type: rings, necklaces, earrings and bracelets.
- Routes by collection, occasion or intent: new, best sellers, gifting, bridal or personalised.
- Product rails that expose name, material, price, variants and save/quick-add actions.
- Both studio product imagery and worn-on-body imagery to communicate detail and scale.
- Brand proof: craftsmanship, materials, sourcing, design story or atelier process.
- Buying reassurance before the footer: delivery, returns, warranty, care, sizing and appointments.
- A comprehensive footer containing help, resources, brand, legal, language and currency.

## Recommended homepage structure

### Essential launch sections

1. **Announcement bar** — One concise delivery, service or campaign message. Avoid rotating several competing offers.
2. **Global header** — Logo; Shop; Collections; Gifts; About/Journal; Services; search; account; wishlist; bag; language and currency.
3. **Campaign hero** — One emotional proposition, one primary CTA and at most one quieter secondary CTA. Use art-directed responsive crops and preserve the jewellery focal point.
4. **Shop by category** — Four to six image tiles: Rings, Necklaces, Earrings, Bracelets and any commercially important specialist category.
5. **New arrivals or best sellers** — A six-to-eight-item product rail with a clear “View all” route.
6. **Signature collection story** — Editorial split layout connecting a collection to its idea, material and making.
7. **Shop by intent** — Gifts, Everyday, Occasion, Personalised, Bridal or price bands, chosen from the actual catalogue and customer demand.
8. **Craft and material proof** — A short, specific explanation of metals, stones, provenance, construction and quality.
9. **Services and reassurance** — Delivery/returns, warranty, jewellery care, sizing, gift packaging, personalisation and appointments.
10. **Newsletter and global footer** — A restrained sign-up proposition followed by complete support, company and legal navigation.

### Add only when real content exists

- Customer reviews or verified social proof.
- Press logos.
- Journal/editorial cards.
- Stores, stockists or appointment locations.
- User-generated imagery.
- Membership or loyalty programme.

These sections should not be filled with placeholder claims. A shorter credible homepage is more premium than a longer generic one.

## Component inventory

### Global navigation

- Announcement bar
- Sticky desktop header and compact mobile header
- Mega menu with category, collection and gift routes
- Predictive search with products, categories and recent searches
- Account, wishlist and mini-bag drawers
- Language/direction and currency controls
- Breadcrumbs

### Discovery and merchandising

- Full-bleed editorial hero
- Split campaign story
- Category card
- Standard product card
- Product carousel with visible controls and progress state
- Editorial collection card
- Filter drawer/sidebar and applied-filter chips
- Sort control and result count
- Recently viewed and related products

### Product card requirements

- Consistent image ratio and uncluttered background
- Product name
- Collection or category, where useful
- Material / stone
- Price or clear “from” price
- Variant or colour indication
- Wishlist action
- Optional quick add only when size/variant selection is genuinely simple
- Entire predictable card area linked to the product

### Product detail requirements

- Studio, detail and worn-on-body imagery
- Zoom plus dimensions/scale
- Product name, price, material, stone, dimensions and weight where relevant
- Variant and size selection with a linked size guide
- Availability and delivery estimate
- Add-to-bag and wishlist actions
- Care, warranty, returns and packaging information
- Craft/material story and recommendations
- Sticky purchase summary on larger screens and a usable mobile purchase action

### Trust and service

- Delivery / returns summary
- Warranty and care accordions
- Material and authenticity information
- Appointment CTA
- Contact options
- Gift packaging and personalisation details

## Jewellery-specific product discovery

Recommended filters:

- Product type
- Metal / material
- Gemstone
- Colour
- Price
- Size
- Collection
- Occasion or style
- Availability
- Personalisation eligibility

Search should understand both product names and natural jewellery intent, such as “gold stacking ring,” “gift under £300,” or the Persian equivalents.

## Bilingual and accessibility requirements

- Set real document language and `dir` values; Persian is RTL, not merely right-aligned English layout.
- Give Persian and English equal typographic hierarchy, spacing and line-height quality.
- Mirror directional icons and navigation motion in RTL where meaning depends on direction.
- Never place essential navigation or copy inside campaign images.
- Keep keyboard focus visible, provide skip links and make controls at least comfortably touchable on mobile.
- Product imagery needs useful alt text; decorative campaign imagery can use empty alt text.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and never make carousel autoplay the only way to consume content.

## Photography and performance rules

- Art-direct desktop and mobile hero assets separately; do not rely on one crop for every viewport.
- Define the focal point before using `cover`, so faces and jewellery are not accidentally cut.
- Use consistent product-card ratios and `object-fit` deliberately.
- Include worn scale, close detail and clean product views.
- Preload only the first meaningful hero image; lazy-load content below the fold.
- Reserve image dimensions to avoid layout shift and supply responsive sources.

## UX principles to carry into design

- Editorial expression belongs in the campaign modules; commerce controls should remain familiar.
- A carousel is supplemental discovery, never the only route to a category or collection.
- Do not hide price, material or variants behind hover.
- Product cards should use one predictable hit area and consistent content order.
- Trust is part of conversion: sizing, delivery, returns, warranty and care should appear before purchase uncertainty becomes friction.
- Dark and light themes must preserve the same hierarchy and accessible contrast; they should not become two different brands.
